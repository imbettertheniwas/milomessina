A little about me

I live for the ten minutes before doors open. That's when a season stops being a mood board and becomes a room full of people holding their breath.

I've spent the last four seasons producing and styling shows across New York Fashion Week — from a fourteen-look debut in a Chinatown gallery to a full CFDA-calendar presentation at Spring Studios. My job is the unglamorous half of the glamour: the casting grid, the dresser assignments, the shoe that has to be re-dyed by Thursday.

Before that I was in the showroom, which is where I learned that a collection lives or dies on the details nobody's supposed to notice. I still pack my own kit. I still tape every hem myself.
